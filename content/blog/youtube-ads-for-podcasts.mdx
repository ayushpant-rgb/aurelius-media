You put a budget behind last week's episode. By morning the view counter has jumped and the episode looks like a hit. Then you open YouTube Studio: average view duration has dropped, the comments haven't moved, and the next episode gets exactly the organic views it always got.

Nothing was wrong with the ad. You bought the wrong thing.

Since 24 August 2026, YouTube [counts a view the moment a video starts to play](https://support.google.com/youtube/answer/12220281), on every format, and in-stream and Shorts ad plays join the public counter [as soon as the ad starts](https://support.google.com/google-ads/answer/2375431). The number under your episode is now the easiest number on YouTube to inflate and the least useful one to judge a campaign by. Podcasts that grow with paid media buy a different event: a subscriber, a follow-on view, an engaged view of a clip that sends someone to the full conversation.

This is the operational playbook for video podcasts, built from Google's and YouTube's help pages (checked on 8 October 2026), Edison Research's 2026 podcast study and practitioners who have published real tests. If you run a brand channel rather than a show, our [YouTube subscriber growth playbook](/blog/youtube-ads-subscriber-growth) is the broader version.

## In a Nutshell

- **YouTube is where podcast audiences already are.** More than 1 billion monthly podcast viewers, and 37% of US weekly podcast consumers use it most often for podcasts (Edison, 2026).
- **Four ways to pay for growth, each optimising for a different event:** Promote in YouTube Studio, Google Ads Video views, Demand Gen with the YouTube engagements goal, and Video reach. Choose by the event, not the format.
- **Promote clips that sell the show.** Cold open on the strongest line, captions, the show's name said early, a clear next step. Keep full episodes for in-feed, where the viewer chooses to click.
- **Target people who already listen to shows like yours:** searches for comparable shows and hosts, placements on similar podcast channels, and your own viewers as exclusions and lookalike seeds.
- **Public benchmarks are thin.** The most cited figures are about $0.05 per in-stream view and one test published in 2026 at $1.00 to $2.19 per subscriber; a widely shared 2026 benchmark table was withdrawn on 4 October 2026.
- **Judge on earned subscribers, follow-on views and engaged views, never the public counter.** YouTube says promotions don't affect organic recommendations, and paid subscribers don't count toward Partner Program eligibility.

<h2 id="why-youtube">Why YouTube Is Where Podcasts Grow Now</h2>

The short version, because you probably already publish there.

- **Reach.** YouTube reported [more than 1 billion monthly active viewers of podcast content](https://blog.youtube/news-and-events/1-billion-monthly-podcast-users/) as of January 2025.
- **Preference.** Edison Research's *Podcast Consumer 2026* found 37% of US weekly podcast consumers name YouTube as the service they use most for podcasts, up from 31% two years ago, [reported by Inside Radio](https://www.insideradio.com/free/video-reshapes-podcasting-as-audience-hits-new-high-study-finds/article_7bcc18a5-45a6-4a2a-98ba-0e137d7a62cb.html). Spotify has been overtaken, and 57% of people who have ever consumed a podcast both listen and watch.
- **The living room.** Viewers watched [over 700 million hours of podcasts on living room devices in October 2025](https://blog.youtube/news-and-events/podcasts-living-room-in-2025/), up from 400 million a year earlier.
- **Audio and video in one place.** A podcast created in YouTube Studio appears on [YouTube and YouTube Music](https://www.youtube.com/creators/create/podcasts/), and audio-first shows can bring episodes in by RSS.

For paid media, that means a podcast platform sitting on top of Google's ad system: you can put a clip in front of people who search for shows like yours, then see whether they subscribed or came back. The same system will just as happily sell you plays that never become listeners. The rest of this guide is about telling the two apart.

<h2 id="campaign-types">The Four Ways to Pay for Podcast Growth on YouTube</h2>

Google has consolidated YouTube buying: Video action campaigns have been folded into Demand Gen ([Kick Ads' 2026 guide](https://www.kickads.co/en/demand-gen-complete-guide) has the timeline) and Video views campaigns are moving to multi-format ads only. Four options remain, and the question for each is **which event the system optimises for**.

### 1. YouTube Studio Promote

[Promote](https://support.google.com/youtube/answer/12199105) is a simplified ad tool inside YouTube Studio, powered by Google Ads. You pick a video, a goal (**audience growth**, **video views** or **website visits**), countries, languages, ages, genders and interests, and a budget with an end date ([setup steps](https://support.google.com/youtube/answer/16869935)).

- **Optimises for:** with audience growth, the viewers most likely to subscribe, per Google's [Promote creator playbook](https://services.google.com/fh/files/misc/youtube_promote_creator_playbook_us.pdf).
- **Counts a subscriber as:** someone who subscribed within 3 days of watching at least 10 seconds or clicking the promotion.
- **Budget:** results are possible from $5, but Google recommends at least $100 and says the system needs 1 to 2 days to learn.
- **Limits:** promotions are managed only in YouTube, so there are no custom segments, placements or exclusions, and Promote isn't available on manager (MCC) or Google Grants accounts ([troubleshooting guide](https://support.google.com/youtube/answer/16869853)). You need an Owner, Manager or Editor role on the channel, and videos must be public or unlisted and at least 10 seconds long.

### 2. Google Ads Video views campaign

Objective **YouTube reach, views, and engagements**, type **Video**, subtype **Video views** ([Google's guide](https://support.google.com/google-ads/answer/13982458)). You set a target cost per view (CPV) and Google buys as many views as it can across skippable in-stream, in-feed and Shorts. Google says multi-format ads can deliver up to 40% more views for the budget, Shorts is only available with multi-format on, and you can add up to 5 videos.

- **Optimises for:** billable TrueView views, which [count](https://support.google.com/google-ads/answer/2375431) at 30 seconds (or the end of a shorter ad) in skippable in-stream, at a thumbnail click or 10 seconds of autoplay in-feed, and at 10 seconds or a tap on the call to action, like, comment or share button in Shorts.
- **Unique strength:** [placement targeting](https://support.google.com/google-ads/answer/2470108). You can hand-pick the channels and videos your ad runs on, the most direct way to reach a comparable show's audience. It's also how a brand buys podcast ads on YouTube against specific shows without a sponsorship deal.
- **Watch-out:** a view is not a fan. You get views at your target CPV whether or not anyone subscribes.

### 3. Demand Gen with the YouTube engagements goal

The option most podcasters have never opened. Once your channel is linked, Google Ads creates [conversion actions for YouTube channel subscriptions and YouTube follow-on views](https://support.google.com/google-ads/answer/12301500), and a Demand Gen campaign with the goal **YouTube engagements** bids for them with Maximize conversions or Target CPA.

- **Optimises for:** subscriptions or [follow-on views](https://support.google.com/google-ads/answer/16041562), the organic views of your channel's videos after someone sees your ad, which is the closest proxy Google offers for "became a listener". Every follow-on view counts, with a 30-day click-through window and a 3-day engaged-view window. Subscriptions are primary by default; you can make both primary.
- **How it serves:** YouTube inventory only, no Google video partners, signed-in viewers only, and the call-to-action button becomes a subscribe button. Google says this can be more efficient for follow-on views and channel engagement than CPV bidding.
- **Targeting:** audience-based: custom segments, your data segments, lookalikes, interests and demographics, with optimized targeting recommended ([setup guide](https://support.google.com/google-ads/answer/13695389)). There's no channel or video placement list.
- **Bidding:** Google's [best practice](https://support.google.com/google-ads/answer/12301500) is Maximize conversions for the first 14 days, then Target CPA once you have enough conversions (it gives 50 as an example).
- **Budget rule:** for Target CPA, Google [recommends a budget of at least 10 times your target CPA](https://support.google.com/google-ads/answer/13695777).

### 4. Video reach campaigns

[Video reach](https://support.google.com/google-ads/answer/10581234) maximises unique viewers with bumpers, skippable and non-skippable in-stream, in-feed and Shorts. It's right for one job: making sure a defined audience knows a new season or a big guest episode exists. It's wrong on its own, because bumpers and non-skippables produce no TrueView views, and Google notes audience lists that depend on one [won't fill from those formats](https://support.google.com/google-ads/answer/2375431).

### The comparison

| | Promote (YouTube Studio) | Video views | Demand Gen, YouTube engagements | Video reach |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| **Set up in** | YouTube Studio | Google Ads | Google Ads | Google Ads |
| **Optimises for** | Subscribers, views or website visits | TrueView views at your target CPV | Channel subscriptions and/or follow-on views | Unique reach or target frequency |
| **Formats** | Chosen by the system | Skippable in-stream, in-feed, Shorts | YouTube inventory only, signed-in viewers | Bumper, skippable, non-skippable, in-feed, Shorts |
| **Targeting** | Country, language, age, gender, interests | Audiences plus topics, keywords and placements | Custom segments, your data, lookalikes, optimized targeting | Audiences and content targeting |
| **Best podcast use** | Cheapest first test of a clip or trailer | Reaching comparable shows' audiences | Subscribers and returning viewers at a controlled cost | Season launches and big-guest episodes |
| **Main watch-out** | No exclusions or custom audiences | Buys views whether or not anyone subscribes | Needs a linked channel and budget to learn | Bumpers and non-skips don't fill view-based lists |

<h2 id="what-to-promote">What to Promote: Full Episodes, Clips, Shorts or a Trailer</h2>

Every podcast has four candidate assets, and they do different jobs.

| Asset | Length | Where it works | Its job | The rule |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| **Full episode** | The whole episode | In-feed, where the viewer clicks the thumbnail | Follow-on views and watch time from people who chose it | The thumbnail and title do the selling. Don't run it as a skippable pre-roll and call the plays listeners. |
| **Horizontal clip** | 30 to 90 seconds | Skippable in-stream and in-feed | Stop a stranger and send them to the full conversation | Cold open on the best line. A view bills at 30 seconds, so land the payoff before then. |
| **Vertical Short** | Under 60 seconds | Shorts feed | Cheap discovery among people browsing | Only the first 60 seconds of a longer video serves in Shorts, with an overlay at 50 seconds linking to the full video. |
| **Trailer** | Short enough to finish | Promote audience growth, Demand Gen | Sell the show, not one episode | For the subscriber goal, Google's Promote playbook recommends a video that shows what viewers can expect from the channel. |

### The first five seconds decide everything

Google's [ABCD guidance](https://support.google.com/google-ads/answer/14783551) starts with attention: jump in, get to the heart of the story faster, use tight framing, and back the story with audio and on-screen text. Its view-metrics page explains why: a steep drop-off after 5 seconds is common in-stream, because that's when the skip button appears.

That rules out what most podcasts open with: theme music, a logo sting, "welcome back to the show", a sponsor read. In our experience the clips that hold attention share a few habits:

- **Start mid-thought,** on the guest's sharpest sentence or the moment of disagreement, then cut to context.
- **Put the stakes on screen** in the first frame for muted viewers.
- **Say the show's name out loud early.** Google's ABCDs for Shorts recommend announcing yourself with audio.
- **Caption everything.** Podbean's [2026 video podcasting tips](https://blog.podbean.com/best-tips-for-video-podcasting-in-2026/) make captions the default because many people watch without sound.
- **End with direction:** the full conversation is on the channel, or subscribe. In Demand Gen YouTube engagements campaigns the button becomes a subscribe button automatically.

**Check the clip against ad policy first.** Per YouTube's [Promote troubleshooting guide](https://support.google.com/youtube/answer/16869853), content featuring a public office holder, by name or image, counts as an election ad and Google recommends election advertiser verification; since 10 October 2025 Google doesn't allow political ads in the EU, so a promotion declared as an EU political ad won't show to EU viewers. Health content can be flagged, financial content may need financial services verification, and emojis, some punctuation or capitalisation in the headline or description can get a promotion disapproved. News, politics, health and money podcasts should check before building a campaign around a clip.

<h2 id="targeting">How to Target a Podcast Audience</h2>

Podcast audiences are easy to describe: they listen to shows like yours, search for those shows and hosts by name, and follow the same guests around. Use that.

### Custom segments from show names and search terms

[Custom segments](https://support.google.com/google-ads/answer/9805516) take keywords, URLs and apps. Choose **People who searched for any of these terms on Google**, which covers searches on Google properties including Google.com and YouTube, and enter comparable show names, their hosts, recurring guests, and the topic phrases your listeners search (for a running show, "marathon training plan"). URLs of competitor show websites reach people who browse similar sites; they don't place your ads there.

A caution from a real test: in former Googler Jyll Saskin Gales's [January 2026 comparison](https://learn.jyll.ca/blog/should-you-promote-your-youtube-videos-or-use-google-ads-a-case-study), a Demand Gen campaign restricted to a custom segment ran at a $70 CPM against $3 for optimized targeting and for Promote, and produced 2 subscribers at $26 each. One small test, but a tight segment can be very expensive. Run it beside a broader ad group, never as the whole campaign.

### Placements on comparable shows (Video views only)

In a Video views campaign, add the channels and standout episodes of comparable podcasts as placements. Google notes popular channels may need higher bids. If you combine placements with topics or keywords in one ad group, ads show on content matching *any* of them, which broadens delivery rather than narrowing it.

Brands buying podcast ads on YouTube use the same route: a placement list of the shows whose audience you want, plus placement exclusions for anything you'd rather not sit beside. That buys ad slots around the show, not the host's endorsement. A host-read integration inside the episode is a sponsorship you negotiate with the show itself.

### Your own viewers: exclude them, then clone them

With the channel linked, build [data segments of your YouTube viewers](https://support.google.com/google-ads/answer/7181409) in Audience manager: membership up to 540 days, pre-filled from the last 30 days, usable once a segment reaches 100 viewers. Use them twice:

1. **As an exclusion,** so subscriber campaigns don't pay to acquire people you already have. Demand Gen supports excluding your data segments.
2. **As a seed** for Demand Gen [lookalike segments](https://support.google.com/google-ads/answer/13541369), which can be built from people who watched your channel. Through 2026 Google is moving these to a suggestion mode: the seed becomes a signal, the old 2.5%, 5% and 10% similarity thresholds stop restricting reach, and the 100-user minimum seed no longer applies. You can opt out by form.

### Geography: decide where a listener is worth paying for

Two 2026 practitioner cost guides put US and UK audiences at 3 to 5 times the cost of viewers in India, Southeast Asia or Latin America ([AMW](https://amworldgroup.com/pricing/youtube-ads-cost); [Fetch and Funnel](https://www.fetchfunnel.com/i-spent-300-on-youtube-ads-and-all-i-got-was-this-cost-guide/)). That gap is how podcasts end up with big paid view counts and no sponsor value. Google's Promote playbook suggests many countries for the broadest reach, which is right for reach and wrong if you sell sponsorships against a US or UK audience. Target the countries and languages where your listeners and your sponsors' customers live, and put any cheaper international reach in a separate campaign so it never blurs your reporting.

<h2 id="measurement">Measuring It: Earned Views, Engaged Views and the Empty-View Trap</h2>

### What changed on 24 August 2026

YouTube now [counts views the moment a video starts to play](https://support.google.com/youtube/answer/12220281) across Shorts, long-form and live; its [19 August announcement](https://blog.youtube/inside-youtube/engaged-views-youtube-explained/) says views count "from the first frame", including a long-form video autoplaying on the Home page. The old definition survives as **engaged views**: clicking to watch, or staying past the initial seconds, excluding loops. Average view duration is calculated from engaged views, and YouTube says the way CTR, average view duration and retention are calculated hasn't changed, nor have recommendations or Partner Program earnings and eligibility.

Add how ads are counted: public views from in-stream and Shorts ads count when the ad starts, while billable TrueView views count later, at 30 or 10 seconds. YouTube may also freeze or adjust public counts and discard low-quality playbacks even when Google Ads billed for them. So the public counter on a promoted episode is organic first-frame plays plus ad starts, and tells you almost nothing about the campaign.

Podcasts now have a baseline. Podtrac analysed 84.2 million views on YouTube podcast channels on 27 and 28 August 2026 and [found engaged views were 64% of views](https://podnews.net/press-release/podtrac-youtube-stats) for episodes in a podcast playlist and 58% for other videos on those channels. Podtrac says its rankings will use engaged views. Compare your paid traffic's engaged share with your organic share and with that baseline.

### The metrics that tell you whether ads worked

| Metric | Where | What it means | What good looks like |
|---|---|---|---|
| **Earned subscribers** | Google Ads, earned actions | Subscriptions on linked channels within 7 days of an ad view; not charged | Grows with spend |
| **Earned views** | Google Ads, earned actions | 10-second views on linked channels within 7 days of an ad view, one per person per video | People watching other episodes |
| **Follow-on views** | Google Ads conversions (Demand Gen) | Organic views after an ad; every view counts | Your main optimisation target |
| **Engaged views vs views, traffic source YouTube advertising** | YouTube Analytics | Share of ad-sourced plays that stayed past the first seconds | Close to your organic share |
| **Average view duration, paid** | YouTube Analytics | Calculated from engaged views | Different from organic is expected |
| **Casual and regular viewers** | YouTube Analytics, Audience tab | [Viewers who come back](https://support.google.com/youtube/answer/13615784) after their first watch | Rising in the weeks after a campaign |

Earned actions only appear when the channel is [linked to Google Ads](https://support.google.com/youtube/answer/3063482), and vanish if **Disable interest-based ads** is ticked in the channel's advanced settings. To compare with YouTube Analytics, use the **YouTube advertising** row under traffic source and allow 48 to 72 hours for Analytics to catch up.

### Do paid views help or hurt the algorithm?

YouTube's [Promote help page](https://support.google.com/youtube/answer/12199105) is direct: "promotions don't impact your channel's or a specific video's organic performance". Recommendations are based on how content performs when recommended organically, not as an ad. So weak paid retention won't damage organic distribution (YouTube warns paid retention and click-through rate may look different because promotions reach new audiences), and paid views won't lift it directly either. The only route from ads to organic growth runs through people who subscribe or return and then watch organically, which is why follow-on views and returning viewers are the numbers to watch.

On monetisation, YouTube says watch hours and subscribers gained through ad campaigns don't count toward Partner Program eligibility; only organic subscribers count. Some 2026 guides, including the WordStream write-up above, say otherwise, but YouTube's help page is the authority. The exception: follow-on views from Promote's audience growth promotions do count toward eligibility.

YouTube states that views and subscribers from Promote are legitimate; keep every paid view inside Google's tools.

<h2 id="setup">Step-by-Step: Setting Up a Podcast Growth Campaign</h2>

The build we'd use for a video podcast with a back catalogue and a Google Ads account. Menu names follow Google's help pages as of 8 October 2026.

<ol className="blog-numbered">
<li><strong>Link the channel and check the privacy setting</strong>
Link your channel to Google Ads following YouTube's linking guide. Then in YouTube Studio open Settings → Channel → Advanced settings and make sure Disable interest-based ads is unticked, or earned actions and data segments won't appear.</li>
<li><strong>Switch on the engagement conversions</strong>
In Google Ads, Goals → Conversions → Summary, open the Engagement goal and click Edit goal. Make Engagement an account-default goal (follow-on views need it) and set YouTube channel subscriptions and YouTube follow-on views as primary actions.</li>
<li><strong>Build your audiences</strong>
In Shared library → Audience manager, create a data segment of channel viewers (up to 540 days, pre-filled with the last 30) and a custom segment using People who searched for any of these terms on Google, filled with comparable shows, hosts and topics.</li>
<li><strong>Cut the creative</strong>
A horizontal 30 to 90 second clip with a cold open, a vertical cut under 60 seconds for Shorts, and a trailer or strong full episode for in-feed. Captions, the show's name said early, a clear ask at the end.</li>
<li><strong>Create the Demand Gen campaign</strong>
New campaign → Create a campaign without guidance → Demand Gen → campaign goal YouTube engagements. Google suggests the channel or playlist URL as the landing URL, so point it at your podcast playlist. Run Maximize conversions for the first 14 days; once conversions build up, switch to Target CPA with a budget of at least 10 times the target (we set that as the daily budget).</li>
<li><strong>Set up two ad groups and the exclusions</strong>
One ad group on optimized targeting with a lookalike seeded from your channel viewers, one on the custom segment. Exclude your existing viewers from both and set location and language in each.</li>
<li><strong>Optional: Video views on placements</strong>
YouTube reach, views, and engagements → Video → Video views, target CPV, multi-format on, with comparable shows' channels and standout episodes as placements. Expect higher bids on popular ones.</li>
<li><strong>No Google Ads? Use Promote</strong>
YouTube Studio → Content → Promotions → New promotion, choose audience growth, pick the clip or trailer, set country and language to where your listeners are, and budget at least $100.</li>
<li><strong>Let review and learning finish</strong>
Promotions can sit in Pending for 1 to 2 days of policy review, then need 1 to 2 days to learn. Change nothing in the first week.</li>
</ol>

<h2 id="test-plan">The 30-Day Test Plan</h2>

This mirrors the most useful public test we found, Saskin Gales's head-to-head of Promote and Demand Gen on one video, stretched to a month so you can see whether paid viewers come back.

| Week | What runs | What you change | What you look at |
|---|---|---|---|
| **Week 1** | Clip A in Demand Gen (YouTube engagements) and in Promote (audience growth), at similar budgets | Nothing | CPM, in-stream view rate against Google's 10 to 15% average, cost per subscriber |
| **Week 2** | Add Clip B to Demand Gen with a different cold open | Only the creative | Cost per subscription per clip; engaged share from the YouTube advertising source |
| **Week 3** | Shift budget to the better clip and route; add Video views placements if you have a show list | Budget split | Earned views and follow-on views per subscriber |
| **Week 4** | Hold steady | Nothing | Casual and regular viewers against the prior month; organic views on episodes published during the test |

At day 30, make one of four calls:

- **Scale** if cost per subscriber is within target, paid traffic's engaged share is close to organic, and casual and regular viewers are rising. Raise budgets in small steps.
- **Fix the creative** if the view rate sits well below Google's 10 to 15% average or paid engaged share trails organic badly. The first 5 seconds are the problem.
- **Fix the targeting** if one ad group's CPM runs far above the others, as the custom segment did in the Saskin Gales test.
- **Pause** if subscribers arrive but returning viewers don't move. Either the audience is wrong or the show isn't ready to keep strangers, and the money is better spent on content first; our guide to [growing a podcast](/blog/how-to-grow-your-podcast) covers that side.

Paid YouTube is one channel among several. For the channel-by-channel picture, free and paid, see our companion guide on [how to promote a podcast](/blog/how-to-promote-a-podcast).

<h2 id="bottom-line">The Bottom Line</h2>

YouTube is where most US weekly podcast consumers now go first, and Google's ad system lets a podcast put a clip in front of people who search for shows like it, then see whether they subscribed and came back. That's the opportunity.

The risk grew on 24 August 2026. With views counted from the first frame and ad plays added to the public counter as they start, buying a bigger number has never been easier or meant less. YouTube is explicit that ads neither help nor hurt organic recommendations directly and that paid subscribers don't count toward monetisation. The only paid growth that compounds is people who become regular viewers.

So buy that: optimise for subscriptions and follow-on views, run cold-open clips in-stream and full episodes in-feed, target comparable shows' audiences in the countries that matter, and judge on earned subscribers, engaged views and returning viewers. <h2 id="faq">Frequently Asked Questions</h2>

<details>
<summary>Do YouTube ads for podcasts actually work?</summary>

Yes, when you buy the right event. A Video views campaign delivers views at your target cost whether or not anyone becomes a listener, so it can look like success while the show stands still. Demand Gen with the YouTube engagements goal, or Promote with the audience growth goal, buys people who subscribe or come back to watch more. Judge results on earned subscribers, follow-on views and returning viewers, not the public view count.

</details>

<details>
<summary>How do I promote a podcast on YouTube with ads?</summary>

Link your channel to Google Ads, turn on the YouTube channel subscriptions and follow-on views conversion actions, and run a Demand Gen campaign with the YouTube engagements goal using a 30 to 90 second clip that opens on an episode's strongest moment. Target custom segments built from comparable show and host names, exclude people who already watch you, and pick the countries where your listeners live. Without Google Ads, use Promote in YouTube Studio with the audience growth goal.

</details>

<details>
<summary>How do I get more views on a YouTube podcast without buying empty views?</summary>

Since 24 August 2026 YouTube counts a view the moment a video starts to play, and in-stream and Shorts ad plays join the public count when the ad starts. So optimise and report on events that need real attention: subscriptions, follow-on views, earned views and engaged views from the YouTube advertising traffic source. Run clips as ads, let full episodes run in-feed where viewers choose to click, and never use third-party view sellers.

</details>

<details>
<summary>What is a good cost per subscriber for YouTube ads?</summary>

There is no reliable public benchmark, so treat single figures as a sanity check. In a one-week test published in 2026 by former Googler Jyll Saskin Gales, Promote cost $1.00 per subscriber and a Demand Gen YouTube engagements campaign $2.19, while a Demand Gen campaign limited to a custom segment cost $26 per subscriber. Build your own baseline from cost per subscription, then check those subscribers come back to watch more.

</details>

<details>
<summary>How do I get more podcast subscribers on YouTube: Promote or Google Ads?</summary>

Promote is faster to launch from YouTube Studio, and Google recommends at least $100 per promotion, but it only offers country, language, age, gender and interest targeting, with no exclusions or custom audiences. Demand Gen with the YouTube engagements goal takes longer to set up but adds custom segments, lookalikes, exclusions, Target CPA and follow-on views as a goal. A sensible first test runs both on the same clip for a week and keeps the one whose subscribers come back.

</details>

<details>
<summary>Should podcast ads on YouTube use full episodes or short clips?</summary>

Use clips for skippable in-stream and Shorts, and keep full episodes for in-feed placements and Promote. In-stream viewers can skip after 5 seconds and Google notes a steep drop-off at that point, so a 30 to 90 second clip with a cold open does the selling. Shorts ads serve only the first 60 seconds of a longer video. A full episode works when the viewer chooses it by clicking a thumbnail, which is how in-feed ads work.

</details>

<details>
<summary>Will YouTube ads for my podcast hurt organic recommendations?</summary>

No. YouTube's Promote help page states that promotions don't affect a channel's or a video's organic performance, because recommendations are based on how content performs when recommended organically, not as an ad. Paid traffic can show different retention in YouTube Analytics because it reaches new audiences. Ads don't help directly either: the organic benefit comes from people who subscribe or return and watch organically. Paid subscribers and watch hours don't count toward Partner Program eligibility.

</details>
