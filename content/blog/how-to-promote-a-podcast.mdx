Here's the number most podcast promotion advice skips. On Buzzsprout, which hosts more than 111,000 active shows, the median show's episodes are downloaded [27 times in their first seven days](https://www.buzzsprout.com/stats). Clear 412 and you're in the top 10%.

Most shows near that median aren't promoted badly. They're promoted *once*: a release-day post to people who already follow the host, then silence. That reaches the existing audience and nobody new.

This guide is the channel-by-channel fix: what each platform gives you for free, what paid placements cost on today's rate cards (we pulled Overcast's and Pocket Casts' live prices on 8 October 2026), when a podcast promotion service is worth hiring, and how to tell whether any of it worked. The discoverability side (keyword research, episode SEO, repurposing systems) lives in our [podcast growth playbook](/blog/how-to-grow-your-podcast). This post is about promotion: putting each episode in front of people who haven't heard it yet.

## In a Nutshell

- **Discovery has moved to YouTube and social feeds.** In 2026 research, 40% of listeners said they found their favourite podcast on YouTube, and 61% credited YouTube or social media. App-only promotion misses most new listeners.
- **Promote in a sequence, not a post.** A week of setup, a concentrated release-day push (the charts read recent listening and follows), then two weeks of clips and outreach.
- **Use the free tools inside each platform first.** Spotify Clips, previews and tracked links; Apple's Social Post Generator, QR codes and trailers.
- **Social works through shares, not ads.** Of listeners who found a favourite show via social media, 60% found it through someone they follow and 33% through sponsored content.
- **Paid is cheap to test, not cheap to scale.** Overcast's own estimates run from $3.80 to $16.94 per new subscriber, a Pocket Casts category spot starts at $124 a week, and Spotify Ads Manager can promote your show from a $15 average daily budget.
- **Nobody can sell you a chart position.** Apple says rankings can't be bought. Hire for strategy, clips, outreach and measurement, never for guaranteed numbers.
- **Judge each episode on first-week downloads, follows and consumption,** against real benchmarks: median 27 downloads in seven days, top 10% at 412.

<h2 id="where-listeners">Where New Listeners Actually Come From</h2>

Promotion is a routing problem: put a sample of your show where people already spend time, then make the hop to "follow" as short as possible.

The audience is still growing. Edison Research's [Infinite Dial 2026](https://ssrs.com/insights/the-infinite-dial-2026/) puts monthly podcast consumption at a record 58% of Americans aged 12 and over (about 167 million people). Its [Podcast Consumer 2026](https://ssrs.com/insights/the-podcast-consumer-2026/) report adds that video overtook audio-only consumption in Q3 2025, when 82% of weekly podcast consumers watched video podcasts against 78% who listened to audio only. Edison says the two have run neck and neck since.

Where they consume has shifted too. In Edison Podcast Metrics for [Q3 2026](https://ssrs.com/insights/number-one-podcast-platform/), YouTube is the platform US weekly podcast consumers use most often, Spotify is second at 17%, and Apple Podcasts is third at 9%.

And where they *discover* matters most for promotion. Sounds Profitable and JAR Podcast Solutions published [discovery research in June 2026](https://soundsprofitable.com/press-release/61-of-podcast-listeners-discover-their-favorite-shows-through-youtube-and-social-media-new-sounds-profitable-research-finds/) worth pinning above your desk:

- **40%** of listeners discovered their favourite podcast on YouTube. Add Facebook, TikTok and Instagram and the figure rises to **61%**.
- Among people who found a favourite show through social media, **60%** found it via content shared by someone they follow. Only **33%** found it through sponsored content.
- TikTok discovery is nearly **seven times** more common among listeners aged 18 to 34 than among those 55 and older.
- **64%** of listeners get podcast recommendations from friends, family or colleagues, and **72%** say they're likely to act on them.

The competition is smaller than the headline numbers suggest. The Podcast Index lists [4,736,257 podcast feeds](https://stats.podcastindex.org/daily_counts.json) as of 8 October 2026, but only 466,220 published an episode in the last 90 days, under 10% of the total. You're competing with roughly half a million active shows, not 4.7 million.

<h2 id="release-sequence">The Release Sequence: Week Before to Two Weeks After</h2>

The biggest upgrade most shows can make is replacing "post it when it's out" with one fixed sequence for every episode. Here's ours. For a brand-new show, run it for the launch with a trailer in the first slot.

Do the one-time setup first: list the show in every major app and on YouTube, give it a page on your own site (or a smart link) with a follow button for each app, and put that link in your social bios, your email signature and the welcome email new subscribers receive. Every promotion below should land somewhere the listener can follow in one tap.

<ol className="blog-numbered">
<li><strong>Seven days before: build the kit</strong>
Cut two or three vertical clips (15 to 90 seconds also fits Spotify's Clip limits) and one quote card. Apple's free <a href="https://podcasters.apple.com/support/4039-apple-podcasts-social-post-generator">Social Post Generator</a> exports artwork in every social size from your show's own art. Create a separate tracked link for each channel in Spotify for Creators. Write your guest a share kit: two clips, the links and a suggested caption. Schedule the newsletter.</li>
<li><strong>Two or three days before: tease</strong>
For a launch or a new season, publish a "coming soon" trailer. Apple's <a href="https://podcasters.apple.com/support/869-featured-make-the-next-great-podcast-trailer">trailer guidance</a> is to keep it to two minutes or less: introduce the show, play the highlights, leave a question open, and end by asking people to follow. On Spotify, set the episode type to Trailer and it sits at the top of your show page.</li>
<li><strong>Release day: one concentrated push</strong>
Email, clips on every platform with that platform's tracked link, the guest's post, the Spotify Clip uploaded. Concentrate it because the charts read momentum: Apple's <a href="https://podcasters.apple.com/support/3146-apple-podcasts-charts">Trending Episodes</a> chart reflects recent daily listening, and Apple's Top Shows weighs listening, follows and completion. Ask for the follow, not the review. Apple says ratings and reviews aren't factored into those charts at all.</li>
<li><strong>Days 1 to 3: test hooks</strong>
Post alternate cuts of the same moment as Instagram trial reels, which go to non-followers first. Reply to every comment while the episode is new, and run a Spotify poll if the topic invites an opinion.</li>
<li><strong>Days 4 to 7: read and reroute</strong>
Open your tracked links. Spotify shows clicks, plays and conversion rate per link, and attributes plays that happen within seven days of a click. Put more effort behind the channel that's converting. Pitch the episode to two or three newsletters in your niche.</li>
<li><strong>Days 8 to 14: the long tail</strong>
Recut the best-performing clip for LinkedIn or Facebook. Drop the episode into a Reddit thread only where it genuinely answers the question and the community allows it. If you have a swap partner, their mention should run in a new episode this week. Then compare first-week downloads with your own last ten episodes, not with someone else's show.</li>
</ol>

<div className="blog-tip">
<div className="blog-tip-title">THE ONE ASK THAT MOVES THE CHARTS</div>

End every clip, caption and episode with the same call to action: follow the show. Apple says follows and completion rates feed its charts while ratings and reviews don't. Reviews still help people decide to press play, but the follow is the ask that compounds.

<h2 id="spotify">How to Promote a Podcast on Spotify</h2>

Spotify's own [promotion page](https://support.spotify.com/us/creators/article/promoting-your-show/) is short: claim your show, build followers, and a link to Spotify Advertising. The useful tools are scattered across Spotify for Creators, and most work whether or not Spotify hosts your show, so claim your show there first.

| Tool | What it does | How to use it for promotion |
|---|---|---|
| **[Clips](https://support.spotify.com/us/creators/article/clips/)** | Short videos (15 to 90 seconds, MP4 or MOV, up to 1GB), one per episode, uploaded on web | Spotify can recommend Clips in Home, the podcast feeds, Browse and Now Playing. Upload one on release day; read impressions and plays under Analytics, Discovery |
| **[Episode previews](https://support.spotify.com/us/creators/article/episode-previews/)** | Short visual previews that play as people scroll the Podcasts and Shows feed; Spotify shows them where you haven't uploaded a Clip | Choose Smart (Spotify picks the most engaging section) or set your own 60 seconds. Lead with your best line, not your intro |
| **[Best Place to Start](https://support.spotify.com/us/creators/article/show-page-recommendations/)** | Recommends one episode to new visitors on your show page | Point it at your strongest evergreen episode, not the newest |
| **Host Recommendations** | Suggests other shows, music or audiobooks on your page | Recommend your swap partners. It's a free cross-promotion slot |
| **[Tracked share links](https://support.spotify.com/us/creators/article/link-sharing-referral-data/)** | Unique links per channel with clicks, plays and conversion rate | Name each link by channel and date. Only links copied from Spotify for Creators are tracked |
| **[Trailer](https://support.spotify.com/us/creators/article/trailers/)** | Episode type Trailer, pinned to the top of your show page | Your pitch to anyone who lands on the page cold |
| **Comments and polls** | Fan interaction on episodes | Spotify's own advice is to ask for comments in the episode intro |

Two settings people miss:

**Your first category picks your chart.** Spotify's [podcast charts](https://support.spotify.com/us/creators/article/podcast-charts-on-spotify/) rank the top 200 shows per country by weekly unique audience, a Trending chart by growth, and the top 50 per category in select countries, using the *first* category in your RSS feed. A precise first category gives a growing show a realistic shot at a top-50 list it can then promote.

**Video matters on Spotify now.** More than [500 million users have streamed a video podcast](https://newsroom.spotify.com/2026-05-21/investor-day-podcast-features-updates/) on Spotify, up nearly 50% year on year. If you record video, publish it there as well as on YouTube.

A useful yardstick sits in the [Spotify Partner Program](https://support.spotify.com/us/creators/article/spotify-partner-program/) rules: at least three episodes, 2,000 consumption hours and an audience of 1,000 on Spotify in the last 30 days (plus Spotify hosting and an eligible market). Even if you never monetise there, "2,000 hours a month" beats "more downloads" as a target.

Spotify also sells ads that promote your own show on Spotify, through Ads Manager. What they cost is covered with the other paid options [below](#paid).

<h2 id="apple">How to Promote a Podcast on Apple Podcasts</h2>

Apple documents how its charts work in unusual detail, and it changes what you should ask listeners to do.

**How the charts work.** According to [Apple's chart documentation](https://podcasters.apple.com/support/3146-apple-podcasts-charts), Top Shows and Trending Episodes measure a mix of listening, follows and completion rate. Ratings, reviews and shares are *not* factored in, though Apple notes they influence whether people listen or follow. Automatic downloads don't count. Rankings "cannot be purchased or sold," and Apple monitors the charts for genuine engagement. Top Shows and Trending Episodes run in more than 170 countries and regions, filterable by category.

So the Apple promotion playbook is: drive follows in a concentrated window, make episodes people finish, and stop running review drives as a ranking tactic.

**The free marketing toolbox.** Apple's [Marketing Tools](https://podcasters.apple.com/support/868-featured-elevate-your-marketing-assets) give every show:

- **Social Post Generator:** pick a headline and background, export every social size. If you're in Top Shows, it offers headlines that announce your rank.
- **Embedded players and short links** that open straight into the Apple Podcasts app.
- **Badges and QR codes** for slides, print and event signage. Apple says the app is installed on over 2 billion devices.
- **[Linkfire smart links](https://podcasters.apple.com/support/5312-link-promote-learn-linkfire):** one link that lets each listener pick their app, with data on listens, plays and follows. There's a free plan, and paid plans start at $9.99 a month.

**Trailers.** Apple distinguishes a coming-soon trailer, a show trailer (your highlight reel) and an end-of-episode teaser for the next one. Keep each to two minutes or less and end on "follow the show."

**What iOS 27 changed.** Apple's [September 2026 update](https://podcasters.apple.com/support/5612-iOS-27-whats-new-for-apple-podcasts) added Search in Show (listeners can search a show's catalogue by guest name, keyword or anything said in the transcript), read-along transcripts and chapters for video episodes, and a redesigned Apple Podcasts app on Apple TV that plays video podcasts. Upshot: name guests and topics plainly in titles and descriptions, because that's what people will search.

<h2 id="youtube">YouTube: The Basics That Matter for Promotion</h2>

YouTube is podcasting's biggest discovery platform. It reported [more than 1 billion monthly podcast viewers](https://blog.youtube/news-and-events/1-billion-monthly-podcast-users/), a figure it first gave in early 2025, and it tops Edison's Q3 2026 platform ranking. We cover YouTube search optimisation in depth in our [YouTube SEO guide](/blog/youtube-seo-guide), so here are just the promotion basics:

- **Be there with every episode.** Full video is best. If you're audio-first, YouTube can [ingest your RSS feed](https://support.google.com/youtube/answer/13525207?hl=en): it creates a static-image video from your show art for each new episode, uploads it automatically and notifies eligible subscribers. It's available in select countries, YouTube says podcast content delivered this way can't contain advertisements, and host-read sponsorships must be declared as branded content.
- **Cut Shorts from the moments you clip for Instagram and TikTok.**
- **Read the right view count.** Since [24 August 2026](https://support.google.com/youtube/thread/433409976/an-update-to-how-we-count-public-views-across-youtube?hl=en-GB), YouTube counts a public view from the first frame of playback across all formats, including podcasts. The old metric now lives in YouTube Analytics (Advanced Mode) as **engaged views**. In Podtrac's sample of 84.2 million views from late August, [podcast playlist episodes kept 64%](https://www.podderapp.com/post/youtube-podcast-stats) of counted views as engaged views. Judge promotion on engaged views, or everything will look better than it was.

Paid promotion on YouTube (Google Ads campaigns that push your best episodes to new viewers and subscribers) has its own playbook: see [YouTube ads for podcasts](/blog/youtube-ads-for-podcasts).

<h2 id="social">Instagram, TikTok, LinkedIn and Facebook</h2>

The Sounds Profitable numbers set the rule for social: discovery happens when people *share*. The clip's job is to make a stranger want the full conversation; the caption's job is to make the next tap obvious.

How each platform earns its place:

| Platform | Who you reach | What to post | The ask |
|---|---|---|---|
| **Instagram** | Broad, visual, interview and lifestyle audiences | Reels cut around one moment; Stories with a link sticker on release day; carousels of the episode's key points | Follow on your main app via a tracked link in Stories or bio |
| **TikTok** | Under-35 listeners: discovery there is nearly 7 times more common at 18 to 34 than at 55+ | One opinion, story beat or surprising fact per clip, captioned, under a minute | Search the show name, or the link in bio |
| **LinkedIn** | Professionals; best for B2B, founder-led and expert shows | Native video clips posted from the host's personal profile, plus a text post with the episode's three takeaways | Comment, then listen via the link in the first comment |
| **Facebook** | Counted in the social share of discovery in Sounds Profitable's data | Clips and episode posts in relevant Groups where rules allow; Reels | Follow via tracked link |

Instagram's [trial reels](https://creators.instagram.com/blog/instagram-trial-reels) are the most useful native tool here. A trial reel goes to non-followers first and stays off your grid. You get views, likes, comments and shares after about 24 hours, and you can set it to share with followers automatically if it performs well in its first 72 hours. Post two or three hooks as trials and promote the winner.

A few production rules that hold across all four:

- **Open on the strongest line, not the setup.** If the first two seconds are "So, welcome back," the clip is already lost.
- **Burn captions in,** so the clip works with the sound off.
- **One idea per clip.** A clip that tries to summarise the episode does neither job.
- **Name the show and the episode at the end,** so a share still carries the way back.

If clipping is the bottleneck, it's the part most worth outsourcing; our [reels editing team](/services/reels-editing) cuts podcast clips for this sequence.

<h2 id="communities">Reddit, Newsletters and Your Own Email List</h2>

These reach smaller audiences than social feeds, but the people in them chose the topic, which is why they tend to convert.

**Reddit.** Reddit's [spam policy](https://support.reddithelp.com/hc/en-us/articles/360043504051-Spam) is explicit: mass-posting repetitive content for exposure is spam, and if your contributions are mostly links to something you benefit from, Reddit suggests you either post less or use its self-serve ads. Each subreddit's moderators decide what counts as self-promotion in their space. Participate first: answer questions in your niche's subreddits, and link an episode only when it genuinely answers the thread and the rules allow it.

**Newsletters.** Podcast recommendation newsletters exist to find shows for their readers. Lauren Passell of Tink Media, writing for [Descript](https://www.descript.com/blog/article/how-to-buy-ads-for-your-podcast-if-you-have-the-money), notes that many accept pitches for free, and that paid classifieds typically cost around $30 to $200 per newsletter. Pitch one specific episode with one sentence on who it's for. Then look beyond podcast newsletters to those in your topic: a parenting show belongs in parenting newsletters.

**Your own email list.** It's the only channel no algorithm can take away. Send one email per episode with a single reason to listen and a single tracked link, and mention the sign-up in episodes. A small list that opens every week can do more for first-week downloads than a large social following that rarely sees your posts.

<h2 id="cross-promotion">Cross-Promotion: Swaps, Feed Drops and Guesting</h2>

Cross-promotion borrows an audience that already has the listening habit. The common formats, as [RSS.com](https://rss.com/blog/read-this-before-buying-ads-to-promote-your-podcast/) lays them out:

- **Promo swaps:** each show runs a 30 to 60 second spot for the other, ideally host-read.
- **Feed drops (episode swaps):** a full episode of your show published in a partner's feed, usually during their break or between seasons.
- **Interview swaps:** each host appears on the other's show.
- **Trailer swaps:** Apple specifically recommends cross-promoting your trailer with partner podcasts.

Swaps fail in predictable ways. The short version of Podder's [August 2026 breakdown](https://www.podderapp.com/post/cross-promotion-mistakes):

1. **Pick partners by audience overlap, not genre.** Ask who their listener is and what else they listen to.
2. **Stay in a similar download tier.** A show far bigger than yours gives you a mention its listeners barely notice, and you can't send enough back.
3. **Run it in a new episode,** not a back-catalogue episode that gets a trickle of downloads.
4. **Name the specific episode** listeners should start with, not just the show.
5. **Use a trackable link or code** for every swap, so both sides can see what happened.
6. **Repeat what works.** One swap is a coin flip; a partner you swap with repeatedly builds familiarity with both audiences.

The Podglomerate's [partner guide](https://podglomerate.com/how-to-identify-an-ideal-cross-promotional-partner-for-podcasts/) adds a fairness rule: trade impressions, based on each show's monthly downloads, so neither side subsidises the other. Agree host-read or pre-produced in advance, in writing.

**Guesting** is the same idea at a deeper level: an hour in front of an audience that already trusts the host. Choose shows by listener overlap, bring one story people can't get elsewhere, and point listeners to the one episode of yours that continues the conversation.

One caution: Sounds Profitable's Tom Webster warns that shows relying mainly on trailers, chart positions or cross-promotion inside podcast apps miss where most new listeners now discover shows. Swaps reach people who already listen in apps, so pair them with YouTube and social.

| **[Spotify Ads Manager](https://adshelp.spotify.com/s/article/Streams-Objective-Onboarding-Guide-US?language=en_US)** | Audio or video ads (30 seconds or less) that promote your own show on Spotify, optimised for streams and priced by auction; 13 markets including the US, UK, India and Australia | Minimum [$15 average daily budget](https://adshelp.spotify.com/s/article/Setting-your-budget-US?language=en_US), or about $250 lifetime | Estimated impressions, reach and frequency as you set up |
| **Ads on other podcasts** | Pre-recorded or host-read spots | [$15 to $30 CPM](https://www.acast.com/en/news-and-insights/how-much-does-podcast-advertising-cost) produced; $25 to $40 CPM host-read; $40+ for business and finance shows | Downloads of the episode carrying your ad |
| **Newsletter classifieds** | A listing in a podcast or niche newsletter | About $30 to $200 per newsletter (Descript) | Clicks |

How to read those numbers:

**A tap is not a listener.** Divide Pocket Casts' starting prices by its own tap estimates and a first-carousel tap costs roughly $0.77 to $2.38, while a lower-feed tap costs $2.00 to $4.14. Pocket Casts reports [subscribes](https://pocketcasts.com/ads) at the end of each run; that's the number to compare with Overcast's cost per subscription.

**Targeting is thin, so fit matters.** Overcast collects no listener demographics, so it won't take ads for non-English, region-specific or gender-specific shows, and it excludes topics such as investment advice, crypto, gambling and alcohol. Pocket Casts doesn't target by region or demographics either, and says its audience skews male, aged 35 to 60, in the US, UK, Australia and Canada. If your show fits, app ads are among the most direct buys in podcasting. If not, spend elsewhere. Spotify Ads Manager is the exception here: its streams campaigns can target by location, age, gender, interests, language and device.

**Other shows' ads are priced per thousand downloads.** At the [$25 CPM Libsyn estimates](https://rephonic.com/blog/podcast-ad-rates/) for host-read spots, a show with 10,000 downloads per episode charges about $250 for one read. Ask for recent per-episode downloads, and pick audience overlap over size.

**Meta and other social ads need a page you can measure.** Social ads can't optimise for a follow inside Spotify or Apple, so send paid clicks to a page you control (your episode page or a smart link page) with tracked app links. Your real cost per listener is cost per click divided by the share of clicks that become plays, which Spotify's tracked links report directly. Promote a clip that has already earned organic shares. <h2 id="measure">What to Measure: Downloads, Follows and Consumption</h2>

Total downloads is the least useful number on your dashboard, because it mixes your back catalogue, auto-downloads and promotion into one figure. Track these instead.

**1. Downloads per episode in the first seven days.** This isolates each release and its promotion. Buzzsprout publishes the distribution monthly; the [September 2026 figures](https://www.buzzsprout.com/stats):

| First-7-day downloads per episode | Where that puts you on Buzzsprout |
|---|---|
| 4,586 | Top 1% |
| 1,007 | Top 5% |
| 412 | Top 10% |
| 96 | Top 25% |
| 27 | Median (top 50%) |

Two cautions. These are audio downloads, so with most weekly podcast consumers now watching video, a video-heavy show will look smaller here than it is. And compare against your own trend first: doubling your first-week downloads is promotion working, whatever the percentile.

**2. Follows.** Apple's charts weigh follows, Spotify's Top Podcasts chart ranks by weekly unique audience, and a follow means the next episode arrives with no promotion at all. Track follower growth weekly in Spotify for Creators and Apple Podcasts Connect.

**3. Consumption.** Apple's charts use completion rate. Spotify's Partner Program counts a stream only after 60 seconds and sets its bar in consumption hours. YouTube's engaged views separate real watching from autoplay starts. Promotion that brings in people who leave immediately isn't working, however good the spike looks.

**4. Attribution per channel.** Spotify tracked links, Apple short links or Linkfire, Overcast's subscription counts, Pocket Casts' end-of-run report and a unique link or code for every swap. Give every channel its own link and "what's working?" answers itself within a month.

<h2 id="bottom-line">The Bottom Line</h2>

Promoting a podcast isn't one post on release day, and it isn't buying downloads. It's a repeatable sequence that puts a sample of each episode where new listeners already are (YouTube, short-form feeds, other podcasts, inboxes), uses the free tools Spotify and Apple already provide, and asks every new listener for one thing: the follow.

The data agrees from every direction. Discovery happens mostly on YouTube and in social feeds, through shares rather than ads. The charts reward follows, listening and completion, not reviews. Podcast-app ads are a cheap test, with published estimates of what each subscriber costs. And the only metrics worth tracking are the ones you can tie to a channel.

<h2 id="faq">Frequently Asked Questions</h2>

<details>
<summary>How do I promote my podcast for free?</summary>

Use the tools the platforms already give you. In Spotify for Creators, upload a 15 to 90 second Clip per episode, set episode previews, pick a Best Place to Start episode and create a tracked link for each channel. On Apple, use the free Marketing Tools (social posts, badges, short links, QR codes) and publish a trailer of two minutes or less. Then run the same release sequence every episode: short clips, one email, a share kit for your guest and a promo swap with a show whose audience overlaps yours.

</details>

<details>
<summary>How do I promote a podcast on Spotify?</summary>

Claim your show in Spotify for Creators, even if you host elsewhere. Upload one Clip per episode (15 to 90 seconds), which Spotify can recommend in Home, the podcast feeds, Browse and Now Playing. Turn on episode previews, set a Best Place to Start episode, publish a trailer, and use tracked links to see clicks, plays and conversion rate per channel. Choose your first RSS category carefully, because Spotify uses it to decide which category chart you can appear on. With budget, Spotify Ads Manager can also run ads that promote your show, from a $15 average daily budget.

</details>

<details>
<summary>How do I promote a podcast on Instagram and TikTok?</summary>

Post short vertical clips built around one strong moment, with burned-in captions and the show name. On Instagram, test two or three hooks as trial reels, which go to non-followers first, then share the winner. TikTok skews young: Sounds Profitable's 2026 research found TikTok discovery is nearly seven times more common among listeners aged 18 to 34 than among those 55 and older. Use a tracked Spotify or Apple link and judge clips by shares and link plays, not views.

</details>

<details>
<summary>How do I promote a podcast episode after it's released?</summary>

Concentrate the push into release day and the 14 days after it. On release day, send your email, post a clip with a tracked link on each platform, upload the Spotify Clip and give your guest a share kit. Apple's Trending Episodes chart reflects recent daily listening, so early momentum matters. In the first week, test alternate hooks and pitch newsletters; in the second, recut the best clip for LinkedIn or Facebook and compare first-week downloads with your own baseline.

</details>
